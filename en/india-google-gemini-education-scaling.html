India is teaching Google how AI in education can scale

Arun Patel · January 26, 2026 · 6 min read

With the highest global usage of Gemini for learning, India is emerging as a key market shaping Google's approach to AI in education. The country's massive scale and unique educational challenges make it a living laboratory for AI-powered learning at unprecedented scale.

When Google launched Gemini with limited availability in India, the company expected modest uptake. Instead, India became Gemini's largest educational market by a substantial margin. Indian students use Gemini for learning more than all other countries combined, conducting everything from homework help to exam preparation to skill development through the AI assistant.

This explosive adoption has made India the de facto testing ground for Google's educational AI ambitions. The lessons Google is learning in India—about what works, what doesn't, and what's possible when AI reaches truly massive scale—are reshaping the company's global education strategy.

The Indian Context

Several factors explain India's embrace of AI for education. The country faces a severe shortage of qualified teachers—particularly in rural areas and for advanced subjects. The coaching industry that prepares students for competitive exams costs thousands of dollars annually, putting quality preparation out of reach for many families. And the education system emphasizes rote learning and test performance, creating perfect conditions for AI-powered assistance.

Language diversity also plays a role. India has 22 officially recognized languages and hundreds of dialects. Gemini's multilingual capabilities make it uniquely valuable as a tutor that can explain concepts in students' native languages before transitioning to English—the language of higher education and employment in India.

Economic factors matter too. At roughly $2 per month for Gemini Premium in India (compared to $20 in the US), AI tutoring costs a fraction of human tutoring. For a country where per capita income is roughly $2,500 annually, this pricing makes personalized education accessible to millions who couldn't otherwise afford it.

"India isn't just adopting AI for education—it's showing the world what happens when AI-powered learning reaches hundreds of millions of students simultaneously. The scale is unlike anything we've seen before."

Usage Patterns

The ways Indian students use Gemini reveal distinct patterns that Google hadn't fully anticipated. Perhaps most surprisingly, exam preparation dominates usage. Students spend hours asking Gemini to explain concepts, generate practice problems, simulate tests, and provide feedback on answers. The competitive exam system that determines admission to universities and government jobs creates enormous demand for AI-assisted preparation.

Homework help is another major use case, but with an interesting twist: students often use Gemini to understand concepts rather than just get answers. The most popular prompt pattern is "explain like I'm five" followed by increasingly sophisticated follow-up questions. Students are treating Gemini as a personalized tutor that adapts explanations to their level of understanding.

Language learning represents a third major category. Students use Gemini to practice English conversation, learn technical vocabulary, and get grammar corrections. Unlike traditional language learning apps, Gemini provides contextual explanations and unlimited practice opportunities, making it particularly effective for intermediate learners who've plateaued with other methods.

The Rural Revolution

Perhaps the most significant development is AI's penetration of rural education. India's rural schools face chronic teacher shortages and limited resources. Gemini is filling gaps that would otherwise require massive infrastructure investments.

Consider a typical scenario: a rural high school with one physics teacher for hundreds of students across multiple grades. That teacher can't provide individual attention to every student. But with Gemini, students can get personalized explanations, practice problems, and feedback regardless of class size. The AI doesn't replace the teacher but extends their reach dramatically.

Offline capabilities have been crucial for rural adoption. Google has developed versions of Gemini that work with intermittent connectivity, downloading content when connections are available and running calculations locally. This has made AI education viable in areas where reliable internet remains unavailable.

Google's Strategic Response

The unexpected scale of Indian adoption has prompted Google to reconsider its education strategy. The company is now developing India-first features that may eventually roll out globally. These include curriculum-aligned content, integration with Indian exam boards, and specialized modes for competitive exam preparation.

Google is also investing heavily in Indian languages. The company recently announced support for eight additional Indian languages in Gemini, with more coming. The goal is to make AI education accessible in every major Indian language, recognizing that English fluency varies widely across the country.

Pricing experiments are another focus. Google is testing ultra-low-cost tiers subsidized by advertising, government partnerships that provide free access to public school students, and tiered pricing based on income levels. These experiments could inform pricing strategies for other developing markets.

Challenges and Concerns

The rapid adoption hasn't been without controversy. Educators worry that AI assistance may undermine learning—students might rely on Gemini rather than developing their own understanding. Some universities have banned AI tools during exams, while others are redesigning assessments to minimize AI's advantage.

There are also quality concerns. Gemini can hallucinate information, give outdated explanations, or misunderstand local context. In a country where exam success determines life trajectories, errors have real consequences. Google is working to improve accuracy, but perfect reliability remains elusive.

Equity questions persist as well. While Gemini Premium is affordable by developed world standards, it's still expensive for India's poorest families. There's a risk that AI could widen rather than narrow educational inequalities if only wealthier students can access the best AI tools.

The Government Angle

The Indian government has taken notice of AI's educational potential. Policymakers are discussing whether to integrate AI tools into public education, potentially through government-negotiated licensing deals. Some states are already piloting AI-powered learning platforms in government schools.

Regulation is also on the agenda. The government is considering guidelines for AI in education, including accuracy standards, privacy protections, and requirements for human oversight. How India regulates could influence other countries' approaches to AI education policy.

There's also strategic thinking about AI as an industry. India sees its massive AI-using population as an advantage in developing AI products optimized for developing markets. The government hopes India can become an AI development hub rather than just a consumer of Western AI products.

What the World Can Learn

India's experience offers lessons for other countries considering AI in education. First, pricing matters enormously. AI education tools only achieve scale if they're affordable for the populations they serve. India shows that aggressive pricing can unlock demand that exceeds even optimistic projections.

Second, local context is crucial. Gemini succeeds in India because it supports local languages, aligns with local curricula, and addresses local educational challenges like competitive exam preparation. Generic AI education tools may fail where locally adapted ones thrive.

Third, infrastructure constraints shape adoption. Offline capabilities, low-bandwidth modes, and mobile-first design have been essential for Indian adoption. Other developing markets likely face similar infrastructure limitations that AI products must address.

Fourth, the teacher-AI relationship matters more than expected. The most successful implementations treat AI as a tool that extends teachers' reach rather than replacing them. Teachers who embrace AI as an assistant see better outcomes than those who view it as a threat.

The Future

Google expects India's educational AI usage to grow even further, potentially reaching hundreds of millions of students in the coming years. The company is building dedicated infrastructure in India to handle this demand, including data centers optimized for AI workloads and research facilities focused on educational AI.

For India, AI education represents an opportunity to leapfrog traditional development trajectories. If the country can provide quality education at scale through AI, it could accelerate social mobility and economic development in ways that would be impossible with traditional education alone.

The broader question is whether India's model can work elsewhere. Countries across Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America face similar educational challenges. If India proves that AI education can work at truly massive scale, the model could spread rapidly—transforming global education in the process.

For now, India remains the world's largest experiment in AI-powered education. Google is learning daily what works and what doesn't, iterating rapidly based on usage patterns and feedback. The company's educational AI strategy is increasingly being written in India, with implications that will be felt globally for years to come.
